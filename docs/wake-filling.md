# CLAIM-1 — Wake-filling benefit of KESTREL-X1, computed in-repo [C]

> **Tag [C] = computed in this repo by `lib/wake_filling.py` from
> order-of-magnitude methods. NOT validated by CFD or experiment.**
> Machine-readable output: `schemes/kestrel-x1/analysis/wake-filling.json`
> (regenerated by CI on every push; a freshness gate fails the build if the
> committed JSON does not match a fresh run).

## The claim (falsifiable)

**At the M0.58 / 25,000 ft cruise point, wake filling as configured on
KESTREL-X1 is worth ≈ 2.1% of cruise propulsive power (sensitivity 1.3–3.0%),
and it is dominated by the single aft BLI fan. The trailing-edge fans at the
wing–body junction contribute ≈ 0.01% — at this scale their placement is
packaging-driven, not physics-driven.**

Falsification path: CFD of the configuration with actuator-disc fans at the two
placements (or a wind-tunnel wake survey at the fan inlet stations) should
reproduce ingested velocity deficits of the same order — ≈ 5% of U∞ at the aft
fan, ≤ 0.2% at the TE junction fans. If it does not, this claim is wrong and
the scheme's fan placement should be redone.

## Why this is the scheme's one claim

The concept combines several published ideas (X-66 truss wing, D8 fuselage,
N3-X DEP, STARC-ABL BLI — all [E]). A list of features is not a design
decision; **the one decision this scheme makes from wake physics is where the
fans sit**. This document computes what that placement is worth, from the
model's own geometry (`schemes/kestrel-x1/geom.py`, shared with `model.py` so
the numbers cannot diverge from the CAD).

## Method (every step reproducible)

1. **Atmosphere (A1):** ISA troposphere + Sutherland viscosity at 25,000 ft →
   ρ = 0.549 kg/m³, ν = 2.805e-5 m²/s, a = 309.7 m/s, U = M0.58·a = 179.6 m/s,
   q = 8,854 Pa.
2. **Fuselage drag (A2, A3):** wetted area of the lofted body of revolution
   (361.6 m²) × turbulent flat-plate Cf(Re_L = 2.56e8) = 1.87e-3, no form
   factor → D_fus ≈ 5,992 N, i.e. **57% of total cruise drag** (10,592 N from
   `sizing.json`). The wide double-bubble fuselage dominates the drag budget.
3. **Fuselage wake at the TE fan inlets (A4, A5):** Gaussian deficit profile in
   span, dV(y) = dV0·exp(−y²/2σ²), momentum-normalized so the deficit flux
   equals D_fus. Wake half-width b = R_max + s·(x − x_nose) with spreading
   s = 0.05 → b ≈ 2.7–2.8 m at the fan stations (x ≈ 0.2–1.4 m, ~21 m behind
   the nose). Peak centerline deficit ≈ 3.2 m/s (1.8% of U).
4. **Deficit at each fan (A6):** dV at the fan's span station y:
   y = 3 m → **0.30 m/s (0.17% of U)**; y = 5 m → 0.005 m/s; y ≥ 7 m → < 0.001 m/s.
   Only the innermost fan pair on each side sees any fuselage wake.
5. **Power saving of a wake-ingesting propulsor (A7):** for an ideal propulsor,
   T = ṁ(V_j − V_in) and P = ½ṁ(V_j² − V_in²). Requiring the same thrust T
   from a streamtube with velocity deficit ΔV (V_in = U − ΔV) versus free stream
   gives **ΔP = T·ΔV exactly** (no small-deficit approximation). Real fans add
   inlet-distortion losses, so treat the % as an ideal upper bound.
6. **Aft BLI fan (A6):** disc r = 0.9 m at the tail cone (x = 18.5 m, 38.5 m
   behind the nose). Turbulent boundary-layer momentum thickness there
   θ = 0.036·x·Re_x^(−1/5) = 29.1 mm. Mass-averaged ingested deficit over the
   fan disc: ΔV = U·θ·P_bl/A_fan = **9.02 m/s = 5.0% of U** — the classic
   fuselage-BLI deficit magnitude.

## Results (baseline: aft fan carries 50% of cruise thrust)

| item | value | % of propulsive power (2,238 kW) |
|---|---|---|
| Aft BLI fan (T = 5,296 N, ΔV = 9.02 m/s) | ΔP = 47.8 kW | **2.15%** |
| 14 TE fans in fuselage wake (ΔV ≈ 0.3 m/s at y = ±3 only) | ΔP = 0.23 kW | **0.010%** |
| **Total wake filling, as configured** | **48.0 kW** | **≈ 2.1%** |

Sensitivity to the aft-fan thrust split φ: φ = 0.3 → 1.30%, φ = 0.5 → 2.15%,
φ = 0.7 → 3.00% (full table in the JSON).

**Translation:** propulsive power saved ≈ fuel saved at fixed efficiency, so
≈ 2% cruise fuel burn [C]. Sanity check against [E]: STARC-ABL reports
single-digit-to-~10% fuel burn for aft fuselage BLI on a 737-class
turboelectric — ours is lower because only half the cruise thrust goes through
the BLI fan and this is a 76-seat regional, not a large-core design. Consistent,
not contradictory.

## What this changes in the scheme (the point of the exercise)

- The **aft BLI fan is the physics-driven element**; give it the largest
  practical share of cruise thrust (the sensitivity table quantifies why).
- The **TE junction fans are not justified by the fuselage wake** (0.01%);
  they are justified by the *wing* wake — which every TE fan ingests at any
  span station — plus packaging. That wing-wake benefit is placement-
  independent and is deliberately **out of scope** of this claim.
- The fuselage being 57% of cruise drag means the bigger lever on this
  configuration is **fuselage drag reduction itself** (narrower bubble,
  aft upsweep), not fan placement.

## Assumptions (all visible in `lib/wake_filling.py` / the JSON `inputs`)

- A1 ISA troposphere, Sutherland μ. A2 fuselage drag = q·Cf·Swet, flat-plate Cf,
  **no form factor / pressure drag** (underestimates D_fus; the wake model is
  momentum-normalized to whatever D_fus is computed). A3 θ = 0.036x·Re_x^−0.2.
- A4 Gaussian wake in span, vertical extent h = 2b. A5 b = R_max + 0.05·(x −
  x_nose). A6 aft fan r = 0.9 m at x = 18.5 m ingesting the full BL annulus;
  TE fan inlets at their model x stations, fan radius from `geom.py`.
- A7 ideal propulsor (ΔP = T·ΔV); distortion and installation losses not modeled.
